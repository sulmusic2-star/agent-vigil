# Would maintainers pay for hosted checks?

The open-source verifier stays free and local. Before building a hosted
service, this test asks maintainers which checks they would use, what failure
they need stopped, and whether a stated price is worth paying.

## Prices being tested

- **Local — $0:** CLI, schemas, signed task records, signed results, and dry-run integrations.
- **Hosted — $49 per month:** 200 checks, seven-day result retention, GitHub status checks, and webhooks.
- **Team — $249 per month:** 2,000 checks, 90-day retention, policy packs, repository trusted keys, and audit export.
- **One check — $5:** one clean-room run with a retained signed result and no subscription.

These are test prices, not current products. No payment path is live.

## What counts as enough demand to build it

1. Ten unrelated maintainers record non-binding purchase intent and name a plan and expected monthly volume.
2. Three unrelated users who agree to the stated price without a custom discount or sales call.
3. After a separately authorized checkout is live, three completed payments or at least $1,000 in verified revenue.
4. At least two paying users repeat the purchase or retain the subscription for 30 days.

Reservations show intent. Payments show willingness to pay. Renewals show recurring value. None should be reported as another.

## What the GitHub issue asks

The issue asks where the check would run, how often it would run, what it must
stop, which result should block the workflow, and whether the stated price is
worth it. It does not collect payment, ask for private code, or require a call.
